Saho

An open learning platform, in Malayalam and English. Saho is a guide and a circle: it helps you work out what to learn next, and finds you people to learn it with.

Saho is in development. Its own site, saho.day, is coming soon.

Who it’s for

Saho is for people learning on their own, at any stage of life or study. Think of a recent graduate doing part-time work, unsure of what comes next, who wants to explore their world, their skills and what’s possible, at their own pace and in their own language.

Saho is not a coaching app. It’s for finding a direction, not cramming for a single exam.

Institutions can use it too, for their own people. A hospital could run Saho to help its staff keep learning.

What it does

A guide

Talk things through in Malayalam, English or Manglish. The guide suggests paths and next steps. It doesn’t lecture.

Circles

Small groups of learners who meet where they are, online and in person.

Paths

Learning paths shared between places, with credit to the people who made them.

Your record

A record of what you’ve learned that belongs to you and goes with you.

Places, not a platform

Any institution (a library, a college, a panchayat, a hospital) can run its own Saho, which we call a place. Places talk to each other, so a learner at a village library can join a circle at a college nearby.

Every learner has one home for their account and record. They decide what each place can see, and they can take that back. Private conversations with the guide stay at home.

One instance can serve every library in a panchayat. A single person can also run their own.

Where it stands

Saho is being built now, and a small pilot comes next. It will be released as open-source software.

See our updates or get in touch if your institution would like to take part.

The name

Saho is a call to work together, named for Sahodaran Ayyappan and his ideals of fraternity and equality.

saho.day reads as sahoday: rising together.
